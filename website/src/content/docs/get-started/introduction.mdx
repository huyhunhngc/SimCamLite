**SimCam Lite** is a free, MIT-licensed macOS application and CLI tool that gives the iOS Simulator a working camera. Pick a photo, a looping video (up to 4K), a generated QR code, your Mac’s webcam, or a test pattern — and every app on the Simulator sees it as the iPhone’s back or front camera.

**You don’t change a single line of your app’s code.**

## Why SimCam Lite?

The iOS Simulator has no camera hardware. `AVCaptureDevice.default(for: .video)` returns `nil`, leaving every scanner, KYC flow, and camera screen unusable without a physical iPhone on your desk.

SimCam Lite was built to solve two gaps in existing tooling:

- **Commercial simulator tools** require paid per-seat licenses, making them hard to adopt across teams, QA testers, or CI runners.
- **Older open-source alternatives** are often abandoned, require embedding an intrusive SDK into your app, or only return a single static image.

SimCam Lite works out of the box with apps launched from the Simulator **and** apps run directly from Xcode with the LLDB debugger attached.

## Key capabilities

- **Any source as the camera**: still photos, looping videos (including 4K `3840 × 2160`), QR codes generated from links or text, live Mac cameras, or a test pattern. Drag and drop any file onto the preview to switch immediately.
- **Independent back and front cameras**: give each camera its own source, or toggle **Same as back**. Adjust fill vs. fit (letterbox), 90° rotation, horizontal mirroring, and frame-exact video pause.
- **Realistic camera profiles**: emulate **Triple** (`0.5× · 1× · 3×`), **Dual Wide**, or **Single** camera hardware at **30** or **60 fps**, with device rotation following the simulator.
- **Deterministic permissions & audio**: test all 5 camera/microphone TCC presets and stream 48 kHz mono 16-bit PCM audio via `AVCaptureAudioDataOutput`.
- **Full CLI automation (`simcam`)**: control sources, boot simulators, launch apps, toggle permission presets, and configure Xcode schemes from Terminal, UI tests, CI, or AI coding agents.

## How it fits together

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  Sources["Sources (Photo · 4K Video · QR · Webcam)"] --> App["SimCamLite.app (macOS Renderer)"]
  App --> SHM["frames.shm (Shared Memory)"]
  SHM --> Target["Your iOS App (Unmodified AVFoundation)"]
```

1. **SimCamLite.app** renders your chosen back and front sources on macOS and writes BGRA frames into a shared memory ring buffer (`/Users/Shared/SimCamLite/frames.shm`).
2. **SimCamLiteLoader.dylib** is injected into iOS Simulator user processes via `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` (system processes are skipped). It loads `SimCamLiteInject.framework`.
3. **SimCamLiteInject.framework** emulates `AVFoundation` (`AVCaptureDevice`, `AVCaptureSession`, `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput`, `AVCapturePhotoOutput`, `AVCaptureAudioDataOutput`, `AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer`, and `AVCaptureDeviceRotationCoordinator`).
4. **`simcam` CLI** communicates with the running app over a Unix socket (`~/Library/Application Support/SimCamLite/control.sock`) so Terminal and GUI always stay in sync.

<Callout type="tip" title="Zero SDK footprint">
  Because injection happens at runtime in the Simulator, your production App Store archive never contains SimCam Lite code.
</Callout>
